# EV / Solar Project

[![Dolphin][Otf1]{ .artR width="220" }][Otf1]

**Context Summary**  
**Date:** 12 August 2026    
**Status:** Investigation stage — no purchase decisions made.

<hr class="section-break strong" />








## Why replace the Hyundai?

Robyn currently drives a **2014 Hyundai i20**, bought new as a hail-damaged auction vehicle for only **AUD $5,500**.

The i20:

- remains mechanically reliable;
- has never seriously let Robyn down;
- is manual transmission, which Robyn specifically preferred when purchasing it;
- has negligible resale value because of its age and pre-existing hail damage;
- is therefore **not being replaced because it is old, unreliable or unfashionable**.

The problem is that it is a **two-door car**.

With three grandchildren now an important part of Robyn and Julia's lives, accessing the rear seat and fastening children into car restraints has become extremely awkward. Kindy runs and grandchild transport have therefore changed the household's actual vehicle requirements.

The replacement requirement is fundamentally:

> **A practical four-door vehicle suitable for transporting grandchildren.**

The i20 will probably **not be traded in**. A relative returning to Australia will soon need a vehicle, and Robyn and Julia are considering donating the i20 to them. This would allow a reliable car with a known history to remain useful rather than accepting a trivial trade-in value.

<hr class="section-break strong" />





## Why consider an EV?

Since a replacement vehicle is becoming functionally justified anyway, Robyn is questioning the wisdom of buying another ICE vehicle and committing to another decade or more of petrol-powered transport.

An inexpensive EV therefore makes sense to investigate, particularly because the household already has rooftop solar.

Financially, Robyn and Julia are currently in a reasonably comfortable position: **not wealthy, but not struggling**, making this a good time to consider a substantial capital purchase if it makes long-term sense.

The EV does **not** need to "pay for itself" to be justified. It needs to:

1. solve the changed transport requirement;
2. represent reasonable value;
3. be affordable without creating financial stress;
4. work well with the household's existing/future energy infrastructure.

---

### Existing Solar Installation

The house already has rooftop solar, installed around **2014**, the same year the i20 was purchased.

The original assumption was that twelve-year-old panels probably needed replacing.

This assumption has now been rejected pending evidence.

Solar panels can remain useful considerably longer than twelve years, and the inverter may be more likely to require attention before the panels themselves.

Therefore:

> **Do not replace the existing solar simply because it is old. Measure its condition and performance first.**

---

### Brief for the assessor

The desired request is essentially:

> We have an existing 2014 rooftop solar installation. We are considering adding an EV and potentially making future household-energy upgrades. Before making any decisions, we want a paid condition and performance assessment of the system we already have, preferably with a written report. We are not currently seeking a quote for a replacement solar system.

Questions should include:

- What is the installed panel capacity?
- What should it reasonably produce now?
- What is it actually producing?
- Is degradation normal for its age?
- Are individual panels failing or significantly underperforming?
- What condition are the inverter, isolators, wiring, connectors and mounting hardware in?
- Are there safety/compliance problems?
- Is there any technical reason to replace the installation now?
- What is a reasonable estimate of remaining useful life?
- Can the existing electrical installation/switchboard accommodate an EV charger?
- Would any switchboard or supply work be required for a 7 kW charger?
- Could the existing system later accommodate battery storage?

The ideal result may simply be:

> **The existing solar is healthy. Leave it alone.**

No solar replacement decision should be made until this assessment has been completed.

<hr class="section-break strong" />






## EV Charger

An earlier provisional allowance of **AUD $6,000–$7,000** for an EV charger was probably much too high.

For a reasonably straightforward installation, a more realistic provisional allowance for a **7 kW smart/solar-aware home charger** is approximately:

> **AUD $2,000–$3,000 supplied and installed**

This could rise if there is:

- significant switchboard work;
- a difficult/long cable run;
- supply limitations;
- three-phase electrical work;
- unusually expensive charging equipment.

Therefore the solar/electrical assessment should establish the condition of the switchboard and suitability for EV charging before obtaining charger quotes.

Important conceptual point:

> **The solar installation does not have to produce 7 kW for the EV charger to work.**

The charger uses household electrical supply. Solar-aware charging can preferentially consume available surplus solar, while the grid supplies additional energy when necessary.

A charger is also better regarded as **household infrastructure** rather than part of the consumable cost of one particular EV. It can service future EVs as well.

The developing specification is:

More specifically:

- rear doors should open sufficiently widely;

- both Robyn and Julia must be comfortable driving it;
- cabin quality matters to Julia;
- Robyn will be the primary driver;
- Julia is likely to drive it as well.

Julia has previously disliked the **plasticky feel of an MG**, although value may ultimately matter more than this impression.

Julia has also raised a legitimate concern about **BYD after-sales support**. This needs investigation at the local-dealer level rather than merely relying upon the manufacturer's warranty.

### Range Requirement — Eddie Test

Range became substantially more important once the normal family-driving requirement was quantified.

Robyn lives around **Redland Bay**.

Grandson Eddie lives at **Deebing Heights**.

Approximate journey:

> **70 km each way / ~140 km return**

Driving time is roughly **55 minutes each way**.

A normal Eddie day could easily become **160–180 km** after local trips, detours, shops, parks, etc.

Therefore the cheapest EV is **not necessarily suitable**.

The BYD Atto 1 Essential, with approximately 220 km nominal WLTP range, was rejected as too marginal for this use.

The working range requirement became:

- ~300 km WLTP = absolute minimum;
- ~340 km = comfortable;
- ~400 km = highly desirable;
- substantially more than 400 km is not automatically worth paying a large premium for.

The objective is not merely to complete the Eddie return trip.

The objective is:

> **Complete an ordinary family day without thinking about range.**

Motorway speeds, air-conditioning, weather, battery ageing and deviations from nominal test range should all be allowed for.

### EVs under Discussion

Note: *WLTP: Worldwide Harmonised Light Vehicle Test Procedure, measuring, among other things, the range of an EV.*

The current market has inexpensive EVs beginning well below the original expected ~$36,000 price. For your use, I'd establish this minimum:

~300 km WLTP: absolute floor.
~340–400 km WLTP: much more comfortable.
400+ km: lovely, but probably not worth paying a large premium for by itself.

As the Eddie run is part of the specification, set the WLTP target higher than “340-ish”. A look at the current Australian market suggests the sweet spot is about $30–35k. Not $24k. Not $45k. Somewhere around $30–35,000 drive-away-ish, depending on the deal.

Why? Your normal long family run is already ~140 km return. You'll want a car where 180 km of real driving is boringly routine, even after several years of battery ageing and with aircon running. That means you'd prefer roughly 340 km WLTP minimum, with 400-ish being genuinely useful rather than specification-sheet jewellery.

## Local Support

Local servicing matters.

BYD has representation in/around **Capalaba**, which reduces the concern that servicing would require travelling substantial distances.

Before buying a BYD, Robyn and Julia should ask the local operation directly:

- Is routine servicing performed here?
- Is warranty diagnosis/work performed here?
- If the car fails, does it remain here or get transported elsewhere?
- How quickly can routine service appointments currently be obtained?
- How quickly are warranty appointments handled?
- Are common parts held locally?
- What happens if the vehicle is immobilised awaiting parts?
- What roadside/support arrangements exist?

The same questions should be asked of MG and Geely.

A manufacturer's written warranty is not the entire after-sales story.

## Physical Test-Drive Method

Specifications alone will not determine the winner.

The first physical test should be:

> **Open the rear passenger door and simulate putting Harriet into her child restraint.**

Assess:

- rear-door opening angle;
- height of rear seat;
- ease of leaning into the car;
- access to belt buckles;
- ISOFIX/top-tether arrangement;
- ability to retrieve dropped objects;
- ease of getting a tired child in/out;
- whether repeated use would be comfortable.

If this is awkward, the vehicle fails regardless of its specification sheet.

Then:

- Robyn drives it;
- Julia drives it;
- both assess seating/comfort;
- Julia assesses cabin/material quality;
- boot practicality is checked;
- controls/interface are tested;
- ride quality is assessed.

Neither Robyn nor Julia should have to rationalise a car they dislike merely because it is inexpensive.

## Current Project Principle

The key change in thinking during the discussion was:

> **This is not replacing a working car merely because an EV is newer or more fashionable.**

The household's transport requirements have changed because Robyn and Julia now play an important practical role in the lives of their three grandchildren.

A two-door vehicle is no longer well suited to that role.

At the same time, the household already owns an ageing solar installation and is financially in a reasonable position to consider future electrification.

Therefore the project is best understood as:

> **Assess existing energy infrastructure → preserve anything still useful → add appropriate charging infrastructure → buy a practical four-door EV that fits present family life.**

No equipment should be replaced merely because it is old.

No technology should be purchased merely because it is new.

**Measure first. Buy what solves the actual problem.**
